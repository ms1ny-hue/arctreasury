The problem

Stablecoin payment companies prefund 24/7. The timing is the trap.

Overfund

Capital sits idle in settlement wallets across corridors and counterparties, dead weight against thin margins.

Underfund

A delayed receivable or a weekend gap and an SLA-bound payout misses. Banks are closed; the obligation is not.

No evidence

Treasury can't prove, before settlement time, that every mandatory obligation stays covered under stress.

Bank cutoffs and weekends make the timing hard. Stablecoin rails settle around the clock, so the decision, not the rail, is the bottleneck.

Why Arc, why Circle

Arc is the complete live execution path. Circle is the wallet that signs it.

Arc

  • USDC-denominated gas and sub-second settlement on a stablecoin-native L1.
  • 24/7 rail funds an SLA-bound weekend payout a closed bank rail cannot.
  • On-chain policy contract enforces allowlist, per-tx cap, expiry, single-execution, replay + deploy-chain guards.
  • Reconciled from chain state: settlement is confirmed by getProposal.executed, never a provider's word.

Circle

  • Developer-controlled wallet on ARC-TESTNET signs the settlement.
  • No raw private key in the deployed application.
  • Execution via POST /v1/w3s/developer/transactions/contractExecution.
  • USDC as both the settlement asset and native gas token.

Under the hood

A control plane, not a slide.

Deterministic engine

Bigint money, stress forecast, versioned policy, optimizer + independent verifier, Settlement Coverage Certificate. 36 tests. Handles arbitrary external datasets via a validated ingestion API.

On-chain + signer

Solidity TreasuryPolicyExecutor (17 Foundry tests, fuzz + conservation invariant). Signer abstraction: Circle in prod, never a raw key. 11 signer tests.

Durable + observable

Neon Postgres with compare-and-set state machine (no double approve/settle), a dedicated indexer with confirmation-depth finality, an MCP server (read/propose-only), and schema-constrained AI that can never move money.

Claim discipline · what we say and don't
